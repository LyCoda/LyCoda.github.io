The operational problem

At KOLN 3D, a manufacturer of metallic surgical implants, post-operative feedback had to connect the implant, the operation and the patient's recovery. A general feedback question could not capture the different functional concerns of a hip, an elbow or an orbital procedure.

The project translated that need into a REDCap workflow for structured patient and surgeon feedback. My work covered questionnaire implementation, anatomy-specific organisation, automated email distribution and training the R&D team to manage the process.

Start with the operation, then the assessment

The form began with patient-specific implant details, serial number, operation location and date, and follow-up date. A surgical-region selection then organised the relevant clinical assessment. Skull procedures included an additional region choice.

The assessment structure combined named orthopaedic instruments with custom post-operative sections. Keeping that distinction visible matters: implementing questions in REDCap does not itself validate a questionnaire, a calculated field or a new scoring interpretation.

Depending on the region, questions captured pain, stability, motion, walking support, daily activities or procedure-specific symptoms. Calculated fields gave each assessment a consistent place in the record, alongside the underlying responses.

The feedback workflow

I recreated the question and response structures in REDCap and configured automated email workflows to support repeatable survey distribution. Organising the records consistently made it easier to follow the same implant through successive feedback points.

Training was part of delivery. The R&D team needed to understand how to manage the surveys, monitor responses and interpret the collection workflow after the implementation work ended.

  1. RecordAssociate the implant and operation with the follow-up.
  2. RoutePresent the relevant anatomical assessment.
  3. CollectDistribute surveys and capture structured responses.
  4. CalculateStore the assessment output alongside its source answers.
  5. ReviewMake the feedback available to the R&D team.

Making sense of the scores

I also prepared a short interpretation reference to accompany the calculated outputs. This mapped each assessment to its own score bands, rather than assuming that the same numeric value meant the same thing across all instruments.

The supplied scoring notes make that need clear: different instruments use different boundaries and category labels. Custom orbital, TMJ and lung-function sections were explicitly marked as self-implemented. They should be understood as project-specific collection tools, with clinical review required before any decision-making use.

The portfolio describes the structure and engineering work. It does not provide a clinical calculator or reproduce completed patient records.

What the work delivered

The deliverable was a structured, repeatable way to collect post-operative feedback and organise it for review. It brought the survey, scoring fields and operational handover into one workflow.

The project materials demonstrate the form structure and interpretation notes. They do not quantify response-rate improvements, time saved or improved patient outcomes, so those are not claimed here.

The wider internship also included production-planning automation with Excel VBA and Power Automate, an internal CNC mould guide supporting six-degree-of-freedom drilling setup, and 3D-printed display models for commercial use.

What this taught me about clinical software

The hard part was translating a clinical process into something consistent enough to operate repeatedly. That meant asking what each field represented, when a question was relevant and who would use the result.

A useful next iteration would make formula verification, instrument-version tracking and missing-response handling explicit, then evaluate the workflow against agreed operational measures. That would allow usability and efficiency claims to be backed by evidence.
